Zakat on Stocks & Investments 2026

Do I Pay Zakat on Stocks?

Yes, stocks and shares are zakatable assets. If you own stocks, you must include their market value in your Zakat calculation, provided your total wealth exceeds the Nisab threshold and you've held the wealth for one full lunar year.

Zakat Rate on Stocks

2.5% of current market value

On your designated Zakat date (commonly the first of Ramadan), calculate the current market price of each stock you own and multiply by the number of shares. Add this to your other zakatable wealth, then pay 2.5% of the total if it exceeds Nisab.

How to Calculate Stock Zakat

Step-by-step:

  1. Check the closing price of each stock on your Zakat date
  2. Multiply stock price × number of shares = total stock value
  3. Add all stocks together, plus cash, gold, and other zakatable assets
  4. Subtract legitimate debts from your total wealth
  5. If the net total exceeds the Nisab (₹595g silver equivalent), Zakat is due
  6. Calculate 2.5% of your total zakatable wealth and pay it

ETFs & Mutual Funds

ETFs and mutual funds are treated the same as individual stocks. Calculate their market value on your Zakat date and include them in your total wealth calculation.

Important: Check Fund Contents

If an ETF or mutual fund holds haram assets (alcohol, gambling, interest-based companies), you may need to pay Zakat on only the permissible portion or avoid it entirely. This requires detailed fund analysis or consulting a scholar.

Dividend & Interest Income

Dividends received during the year are zakatable and should be added to your total wealth on your Zakat date. However, be mindful that interest income (Riba) is prohibited in Islam and should not be accumulated.

Bonds & Fixed Income Securities

Conventional bonds that earn interest (Riba) are prohibited in Islam. However, Islamic bonds (Sukuk) are permissible and their market value is zakatable using the same 2.5% rate.
