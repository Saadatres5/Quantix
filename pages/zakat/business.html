Zakat on Business Inventory & Assets 2026

Business Inventory & Stock

If you own a business, all inventory held for sale is zakatable. This includes merchandise, raw materials, finished goods, and stock held in warehouses or retail locations.

Zakat Rate on Business Inventory

2.5% of fair market value

On your Zakat date, calculate the current fair market value of all unsold inventory. Fair market value means the price at which it would sell if offered for sale on that date.

How to Value Business Inventory

For Newly Purchased Stock

Value at cost price (wholesale or purchase price) on your Zakat date, or at current market retail price — whichever is lower.

For Obsolete or Damaged Stock

Value at actual selling price on your Zakat date. Old or damaged merchandise should not be valued at original cost, but at what it would realistically sell for.

For Perishable Goods

Calculate on your Zakat date. Any inventory that has perished or expired by that date is not zakatable.

Complete Calculation Example

Scenario: You own a retail store. On your Zakat date (1st Ramadan):

  • Clothing on shelves: £5,000
  • Shoes in stockroom: £2,500
  • Damaged/sale items: £300
  • Total inventory value: £7,800
  • Zakat due: £7,800 × 2.5% = £195

Business Receivables (Money Owed to You)

If customers owe you money for goods sold on credit, these receivables are zakatable at their full amount if you reasonably expect to collect them.

Doubtful Debts

If you're uncertain whether a customer will pay, you may exclude that amount from your Zakat calculation, but it's better to be cautious and include it.

Business Equipment & Property

Equipment in Use

Equipment you actively use in the business (machinery, computers, furniture) is typically not zakatable if it's being used for production. However, if it's stock held for sale (e.g., a shop selling furniture), then it's zakatable.

Investment Property

Property held for investment or speculation is zakatable at its fair market value. Land and buildings held for business operations (store, factory, office) may not be zakatable if not intended for sale.

Ask a Scholar

The treatment of business equipment and property varies by school of Islamic jurisprudence. Consult with a qualified Islamic scholar for your specific situation.

Business Expenses & Debts

You may deduct legitimate business debts and liabilities from your total business value before calculating Zakat. This includes loans, payables to suppliers, and accrued expenses.
